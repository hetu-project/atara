# Institutional roles

Alongside the node ladder, three roles are held by licensed institutions. They post assets outside the system and are governed by contract law and courts, not by protocol code.

| Role | Function | Posts | Earns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ledger seat (about 100, chartered) | Validates the ledger; hourly batch finality of net positions | Assets held outside the system | Seat income, set by the rulebook |
| Clearing member | Net settlement, margin management, fiat rails. Typically OTC desks and banks | A licence and a member contract, enforced in sovereign courts | The ramp and FX spread on fiat legs stays with the member, plus clearing income |
| Underwriting member | Credit lines, pricing of rollback risk, settlement insurance | Capital | Premiums and spread. A balance-sheet business, not a consensus role |

## Two tracks, two rulebooks

The dividing line is what a participant posts. A participant that posts system assets, AIUSD and reputation, is a node and is governed by code. A participant that posts assets outside the system is a member and is governed by law. Code handles everything that can be automated; law handles everything that has to be underwritten.

Ledger seats and members never enter the node ladder, and nodes never take on member liabilities.

## Holding both

An institution may hold a member role and also operate a node, through a separately established agent entity. The two are kept on separate books. An OTC desk, for example, may act as a clearing member for the fiat leg of its trades and run a Light worker for the stablecoin leg.

## Fees on the institutional side

Member fees and seat fees go to the Reserve. The FX spread on fiat rails stays with the clearing member that operates the rail: the protocol sells the right to operate a rail, not the rail's revenue.
