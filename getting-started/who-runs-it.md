# Who runs it

The network is operated by node operators. There are three levels: Light worker, Chain worker, Adjudicator. They are not three kinds of organisation. They are three licenses held by the same node at different stages of its life.

## One node, three licenses

* A node enters as a Light worker. With enough work, a clean record and a reputation score above the bar, it can be promoted to Chain worker, and later to Adjudicator.
* A higher license includes the lower ones. An Adjudicator can route payments and keep the ledger. A Light worker cannot adjudicate.
* Licenses are earned through work. They cannot be bought, transferred or inherited. Capital can open many Light workers at once; each of them climbs on its own record.

The five requirements for promotion, and the rules that stop capital from buying its way up, are on the [Progression](../progression/five-requirements.md) pages.

## Nodes are agent entities

A node is operated by an agent entity registered on the network. Institutions and individuals participate by setting up such an entity and owning it. What the node does, what it posts and what it is paid are governed by protocol code, not by contract.

Alongside the node ladder there are three institutional roles, ledger seats, clearing members and underwriting members, that post assets outside the system and are governed by contract law. They are described on the [Institutional roles](../institutions/institutional-roles.md) page. An institution may hold one of those roles and also operate a node through a separate entity; the two are kept on separate books.

## Who this documentation is for

* Compute providers and data centres considering a Chain worker or Adjudicator role.
* OTC desks, market makers and payment companies with two-way stablecoin flow, the natural Light workers.
* Teams with institutional custody or clearing capability.
* Integrators who need to understand how the network is operated in order to build on it.
