Where the defaults come from.

A model that prices default needs real defaults to learn from. Simulated rounds and wallet activity signals do not produce them. Completed settlements do.

Every trade runs the same five steps. Each one leaves a labelled record: who delivered, who was late, who defaulted, with a real amount attached.

What makes a large trade hard.

Someone has to send first.

Whoever moves first carries the whole trade until the other side performs. Which is why large trades end up inside a small circle of people you already know, at whatever price that circle offers.

Neither side sends to the other. The digital leg comes into our custody and the fiat goes bank to bank. The seller does move first, but to us, not to the buyer.

Release runs on the bank receipt rather than on the buyer’s confirmation, so a counterparty who has been paid and refuses to confirm cannot stall the trade. Neither party learns who the other is, which also means there is nothing to route around next time.

Live

One tainted transfer freezes the account.

Funds arrive carrying a history you cannot see, the receiving account is frozen, and the loss can be the entire amount. Checking it yourself is not realistic.

We screen twice, not once. The wallet is traced through multiple hops before that counterparty ever reaches the book, and the specific transaction that lands in custody is screened again on arrival.

Anything failing the second screen goes back the way it came. It never reaches you, and the trade unwinds under terms agreed in advance.

Live

Your capital is safe because it is stuck.

Full escrow works, and it costs the same on every trade whether the counterparty was ever going to be a problem. Two million held for thirty days is roughly eight thousand in carry, earning nothing.

Post what the risk is worth. The engine estimates how likely this counterparty is to fail on this payment and sizes the collateral to match. A counterparty with a record posts a fraction, and the rest of the money keeps working.

The first two problems are solved by custody and verification, which run today. This one can only be solved by pricing, which is why the engine exists.

In build

Questions we get first.

Is the credit engine live?
No. It is in build and it is not extending credit to anyone. What runs today is the settlement desk, and the records its completed trades produce are what the engine will learn from. Everything on this site is labelled with its actual status, including this.
Are you a payment rail?
No. Settlement runs on existing rails, chains and banks. We build the credit and conditions layer above them, which is why we can sit on top of a rail rather than asking anyone to migrate to a new one.
How is this different from fraud detection?
Fraud detection is binary: allow or block. It stops bad payments and frees no capital. A credit engine outputs a price instead, which is a different kind of answer: how much collateral, what fee, what recourse. Blocking the bad ones is table stakes. Pricing the good ones is the product.
How is this different from identity or reputation systems?
Identity proves who a counterparty is. Reputation ranks how they have behaved. A reputation tier can shorten a lock or raise a ceiling, but it does not reduce the amount you have to post. The amount is the number a treasury actually needs, and that is what we compute.
What data trains the model?
Settlement performance from trades our own desk completes: delivery, timing, disputes, outcomes, each with a real amount attached. First-party records of real obligations, rather than simulated rounds or behaviour inferred from wallet activity.
What can I use today?
The settlement desk. Send a size and a side and you will get a specific answer: whether we have the other side, what documents are needed, and what it costs. If we cannot do your trade, that comes back in the first reply.

Send us a hard trade.

A size, a side, and a counterparty you do not trust yet. If the desk can settle it we will tell you exactly how. If it cannot, you will know in the first reply.
